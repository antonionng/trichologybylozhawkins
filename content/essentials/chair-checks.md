## Module 1: Recognising Common Scalp Concerns

*(Lessons 1-2)*

### Q1.1: MULTIPLE_CHOICE
**Stem:** A regular client says, "My scalp is on fire today." You part the hair and see diffuse redness with no broken skin. What would you say next?

**Options:**  
A. "That's definitely psoriasis. You need steroids."  
B. "Would it be alright if we keep today's service gentler and avoid anything that might sting? I'm noticing some redness along the parting."  
C. "It's nothing. Everyone gets itchy scalps."  
D. "This shampoo cures inflammation in one wash."

**Correct:** B  
**Explanation:** Permission plus plain observation keeps you in scope, lowers alarm, and adapts the service. Naming a medical diagnosis or promising a cure is outside Essentials practice.

### Q1.2: MULTIPLE_CHOICE
**Stem:** On the cape you see many hairs with visible bulbs, and density looks generally even with no bald patches. The client had flu about three months ago. Which picture fits best for your chair language?

**Options:**  
A. Breakage from bleach  
B. Patchy loss needing immediate colour correction  
C. Increased shed from the root (a TE-type timing story is possible; still avoid firm diagnosis)  
D. Traction only at the nape

**Correct:** C  
**Explanation:** Bulbs point to shed from the root. Delayed timing after illness is a common TE conversation, but stylists describe and refer when severe or uncertain rather than diagnose.

### Q1.3: TRUE_FALSE
**Stem:** Short snapped lengths without bulbs, rough ends, and a full-looking scalp usually mean medical patchy alopecia rather than breakage.

**Options:**  
A. True  
B. False

**Correct:** B (False)  
**Explanation:** That picture is classic breakage. Treating breakage as disease confuses care. Adjust chemistry and handling; refer if true root shed or patches also appear.

### Q1.4: MULTIPLE_CHOICE
**Stem:** You notice thinning along the temples and short broken hairs in a band where a tight ponytail sits daily. What would you say next?

**Options:**  
A. "You have androgenetic alopecia. Nothing will help."  
B. "I'm seeing stress along this hairline. Looser tension and rotating styles would be kinder while we keep an eye on it."  
C. "Pull harder so we train the hairline."  
D. "Ignore it; edges always do that."

**Correct:** B  
**Explanation:** Traction clues call for observational language and practical easing of pull. Firm AGA diagnosis from the chair is out of scope; established or distressing loss still warrants referral.

### Q1.5: MULTIPLE_CHOICE
**Stem:** A client has a smooth, round bald patch that appeared quickly. What is the safest next step?

**Options:**  
A. Sell a growth serum and rebook colour  
B. Diagnose alopecia areata on the spot  
C. Soften the service if needed, note plain observations, and refer  
D. Scrub the patch to stimulate growth

**Correct:** C  
**Explanation:** Patchy loss is a red flag. Stylists do not diagnose cause. Comfort, documentation in observation words, and referral are clinically safer.

## Module 2: Client Conversations That Build Trust

*(Lessons 3-4)*

### Q2.1: MULTIPLE_CHOICE
**Stem:** You want to raise a scalp concern. What would you say next?

**Options:**  
A. "You've got a serious scalp disease."  
B. "Would it be alright if I mention something I'm noticing on the scalp?"  
C. "Everyone can hear this: your flakes are bad."  
D. "No need to talk; just buy this."

**Correct:** B  
**Explanation:** Permission-first openings protect dignity and consent. Public or alarmist framing damages trust.

### Q2.2: MULTIPLE_CHOICE
**Stem:** A client asks, "Will this product grow it all back?" after pattern thinning at the parting. What would you say next?

**Options:**  
A. "Yes, guaranteed full regrowth in two weeks."  
B. "Based on what I'm seeing, this can support the appearance of density and kinder care. It won't treat an underlying medical pattern on its own. If you want clearer answers, a trichologist or GP review helps."  
C. "Only bleach will fix it."  
D. "Stop washing forever."

**Correct:** B  
**Explanation:** Structured honesty (what it may support / what it will not do) is confident and ethical. Cure claims are out.

### Q2.3: TRUE_FALSE
**Stem:** On a non-broken sensitive scalp, stacking many strong actives in one visit is usually safer than a simpler, gentler routine.

**Options:**  
A. True  
B. False

**Correct:** B (False)  
**Explanation:** Less is often safer on sensitive scalps. Match recommendations to the observed picture and avoid irritation experiments.

### Q2.4: MULTIPLE_CHOICE
**Stem:** Mid-consultation the scalp looks pustular and painful. Retail is on the desk. What would you say next?

**Options:**  
A. "Let's still do the full scalp scrub and sell three products."  
B. "I'm going to keep things very gentle today. This looks like something a clinician should check rather than something we manage with retail alone."  
C. "It's fine; push through the pain."  
D. "I can prescribe an antibiotic."

**Correct:** B  
**Explanation:** Painful or pustular pictures are stop-and-refer moments. Pause selling; stay in scope.

### Q2.5: MULTIPLE_CHOICE
**Stem:** Which closing best protects trust after a shed conversation?

**Options:**  
A. "Don't worry about it, ever."  
B. "I'll look after what we can safely do here, and I'll help you find the right next step if this needs more than salon care."  
C. "Only I can fix this."  
D. "Come back when it's bald."

**Correct:** B  
**Explanation:** It validates salon support and keeps referral available without scare tactics or overclaiming.

## Module 3: Know Your Boundaries

*(Lesson 5)*

### Q3.1: MULTIPLE_CHOICE
**Stem:** Which task is inside Essentials scope for a stylist?

**Options:**  
A. Diagnosing thyroid disease from hair texture  
B. Interpreting blood tests  
C. Plain-language observation, service adaptation, and referral when needed  
D. Prescribing medicated treatments

**Correct:** C  
**Explanation:** Observation, adaptation, light education, and referral are in scope. Medical diagnosis and prescribing are not.

### Q3.2: TRUE_FALSE
**Stem:** Referral means you have failed as a stylist.

**Options:**  
A. True  
B. False

**Correct:** B (False)  
**Explanation:** Referral is professionalism. Clear boundaries often deepen trust rather than lose clients.

### Q3.3: MULTIPLE_CHOICE
**Stem:** A client asks, "So what have I got?" after you notice a widening parting. What would you say next?

**Options:**  
A. "You definitely have AGA."  
B. "I can tell you what I'm seeing and how we'll adapt today. Naming a medical condition needs a clinician."  
C. "It's cancer until proven otherwise."  
D. "Nothing. Next client."

**Correct:** B  
**Explanation:** Separates observation from diagnosis and keeps language clinically safer.

### Q3.4: MULTIPLE_CHOICE
**Stem:** Which is the best brief note after a red-flag patch?

**Options:**  
A. "Client has alopecia areata / psoriasis / infection (confirmed)."  
B. "Observed smooth round bald patch at left crown; service softened; advised trichologist or GP review; client given Academy/clinic pathway info."  
C. "Ignored; sold growth oil."  
D. "Told client to stop seeing doctors."

**Correct:** B  
**Explanation:** Observation words, action taken, and referral advice are documentable and scope-safe. Firm diagnostic labels from the chair are not.

### Q3.5: MULTIPLE_CHOICE
**Stem:** Where does Knutsford clinic care sit relative to the Academy?

**Options:**  
A. Academy diagnoses clients online with no clinician  
B. Academy is education for professionals; Loz sees clients in Knutsford via the clinic pathway linked through the Academy loop (trichologyacademy.co.uk)  
C. Stylists should claim they are Knutsford clinicians after this course  
D. Referral pathways should never be mentioned

**Correct:** B  
**Explanation:** Keep education and clinic distinct. Mention the pathway lightly when referral fits; do not blur roles.
