## One-pager 1: Scalp concern quick screen + when to refer

**Module 1 handout**  
**Title:** Scalp & hair quick screen (salon chair)

### Before you shampoo (about 10 seconds)
- Part in sections under good light
- Note: redness, flake type, oil/build-up, tenderness, broken skin
- Note hair picture: shed with bulbs, breakage without bulbs, pattern thinning, patchy loss, traction bands

### Common salon pictures (observe, do not diagnose)
- **Sensitive / inflamed:** tight, hot, itchy, red; soften service
- **Flake:** dry powdery vs oily stuck scale; describe what you see
- **Build-up / oiliness:** coated roots, flat styles; clarify gently when appropriate
- **Traction clues:** edge thinning, short broken hairs in pull zones, tenderness
- **Increased shed (root):** bulbs on cape; ask about recent triggers and timing (illness, stress, medication change, postpartum lag weeks to months later). TE is a training term; avoid firm chair diagnosis
- **Breakage:** snapped lengths, rough cuticle, scalp density may still look full
- **Pattern thinning:** widening parting, finer crown/temples (AGA is clinical language; use observation words in salon)
- **Patchy loss:** sudden bare areas, smooth or irregular patches = red flag

### Soften the service when
- Scalp looks red, sore, or easily irritated
- Client reports burn or sting with products
- Traction zones are tender

### Stop and refer when
- Patchy bald areas
- Sudden severe shed
- Weeping, broken, pustular, or rapidly spreading scalp
- Pain out of proportion
- You are unsure

### Chair line (safe)
"I'm noticing [plain observation]. We'll keep today gentle. If this stays heavy, patchy, or painful, a trichologist or GP should check it."

## One-pager 2: Conversation / language crib sheet

**Module 2 handout**  
**Title:** Chair language crib sheet

### Open (permission first)
- "Would it be alright if I mention something I'm noticing on the scalp?"
- If no: respect, still adapt quietly for comfort

### Describe (observation only)
- "I'm seeing some redness along the parting."
- "There's a bit more shed on the cape than last visit."
- "The parting looks a little wider than I remember."
- "I'm seeing stress along this hairline where the ponytail sits."

### Ask (one or two questions)
- "How long have you been noticing that?"
- "Has anything changed recently with health, stress, colour, or styling?"

### Match the picture
- **Sensitive:** "We'll keep today's service gentle and avoid anything that might sting."
- **Flake / build-up:** "We can look at a clearer cleanse and a lighter root routine."
- **Shed:** "Some increase can follow stress or illness a couple of months later. If it's heavy or patchy, I'd rather you had it checked properly."
- **Breakage:** "This looks more like snap through the lengths than loss from the root. We can ease chemistry and heat."
- **Pattern:** "We can style and colour in a way that is kinder to the scalp. Clearer answers need a clinician."
- **Traction:** "Looser tension and rotating styles would be kinder while we monitor this."

### Recommend with limits
Use: "Based on what I'm seeing, I'd suggest… The reason is… What it won't do is…"
- Support comfort, manageability, appearance of density, less breakage
- Do not claim cure, guaranteed regrowth, or medical treatment

### Avoid
- Miracle promises
- On-the-spot disease names as fact
- Dismissing real worry
- Loud floor announcements about someone's scalp

### Close
"I'll look after what we can safely do here, and I'll help you find the right next step if this needs more than salon care."

### Referral wording
"I'd like you to have this checked by a trichologist or your GP. I can keep salon care gentle in the meantime."  
Pathway: trichologyacademy.co.uk (education) and Knutsford clinic via the Academy loop when client assessment is needed.

## One-pager 3: Scope + referral pathway

**Module 3 handout**  
**Title:** Scope of practice & referral pathway

### In scope (Essentials stylist)
- Observe hair and scalp during normal services
- Describe findings in plain English
- Adapt water temperature, products, tension, and chemical plans for comfort and integrity
- Recognise common pictures (sensitive scalp, flake/build-up, traction clues, shed vs breakage, pattern vs patch)
- Recommend supportive products/services without cure claims
- Guide toward trichologist or GP when needed
- Note observations factually for continuity

### Out of scope
- Diagnosing medical disease
- Interpreting blood tests
- Prescribing or advising prescription medicines
- Cure claims for alopecia, psoriasis, infection, autoimmune disease, and similar
- Continuing irritating or invasive steps on broken / severely inflamed scalp
- Ignoring red flags to protect a sale

### Red flags (stop, soften, refer)
- Patchy bald areas
- Sudden severe shed
- Painful, weeping, or pustular scalp
- Rapidly spreading inflammation
- Possible scarring appearance
- Any case where you feel out of your depth

### Referral script
"I'd like you to have this checked by a trichologist or your GP. I can keep your salon care gentle in the meantime."

### Documentation (brief)
- What you saw (observation words only)
- What you adapted today
- Referral advice given
- Avoid firm diagnostic labels you cannot stand behind

### Academy vs clinic (keep distinct)
- **The Trichology Academy** (trichologyacademy.co.uk): education for professionals
- **Clinic:** Loz sees clients in Knutsford; access via the Academy loop when referral fits
- Completing Essentials does not make a stylist a clinician

### Professional reminder
Referral is not failure. Clear boundaries protect clients and deepen trust.
